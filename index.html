About Me

I am a final-year PhD candidate in Astronomy at Saint Mary’s University, where I work with Dr. Ivana Damjanov and Dr. Marcin Sawicki in the Extragalactic Astronomy group. Research

Galaxies do not grow in isolation. Their mass growth and structural evolution are shaped by external interactions with neighboring galaxies and the intergalactic material in their surrounding large-scale cosmic environments. Galaxy stellar haloes, predicted to assemble through hierarchical accretion, retain imprints of past interactions due to long dynamical timescales in galaxy outskirts. Observations tracing galaxy stellar halo evolution enables us to probe how environment shapes galaxy assembly over time. My research uses large multiwavelength photometric datasets and samples of galaxies to study the buildup of galaxy stellar haloes and probe how externally driven galaxy assembly varies across different populations, environments, and cosmic time.

Tracing stellar halo assembly through extended radial light and color profile analysis

To study galaxy stellar halo material, I analyze the faint extended emission throughout galaxy outskirts, since light traces stellar mass. I extract rest-frame surface brightness and color profiles of galaxies using deep photometric imaging data from the ground-based CLAUDS and HSC-SSP surveys. These extractions are made possible for huge samples of galaxies through a Python package developed by our group called GalPRIME. This employs automated elliptical isophote analysis (Jedrzejewski 1987), combined with source masking, 2D background subtraction, and corrections for PSF-dispersed light. I then study population trends in galaxy stellar halo assembly by quantifying the evolution in median light and color profiles and comparing measurements across different galaxy subpopulations and environments. For results of analyses on stellar halo evolution, see my publication list below.

Technical Skills & Software

My research involves performing data analysis on large astronomical datasets. I am proficient in Python, R, and Fortran for scientific analysis, as well as Bash for Unix shell scripting and HTML5/CSS for website development. I have experience developing parallel computing workflows and running large-scale analyses on high-performance computing systems, including submitting and managing jobs via SLURM on clusters operated by the Digital Research Alliance of Canada.

I contributed to the development and testing of GalPRIME, a parallelized Python suite for galaxy image analysis, led by Harrison Souchereau (Yale University). GalPRIME is designed to extract one-dimensional surface brightness profiles with automatic masking of interloping sources and two-dimensional background subtraction, and to assess the quality of extracted profiles through comparisons with simulated models.
